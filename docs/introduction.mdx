## Is it a protocol?

NoirName is a privacy-focused protocol that bridges ENS identity from Ethereum L1 to Aztec L2. Users prove they own an ENS name without revealing which wallet holds it, or linking the proof to their on-chain history.

A public ownership check on L1 is paired with a secret-gated claim on Aztec: verifiable ENS ownership on one side, full transaction privacy on the other, with nothing in between that can deanonymize the user.

## How it changes the game?

ENS is the default identity layer for Ethereum, but every use of a name today ties an action back to a public wallet. That breaks anything that requires privacy: voting, donations, membership, anonymous speech, or reputation that must not leak balances and history.

NoirName separates ownership from action. Proof of ownership happens once, publicly, on L1. Every action after that happens on Aztec, where the proof is available to contracts but the actor is not. Developers get a drop-in ENS check; users get a durable, anonymous identity they control with a secret.

Common use cases: private whitelists and token gates, anonymous social features, private donations and grants, Sybil-resistant voting, and reputation or rewards that follow the name instead of the wallet.
